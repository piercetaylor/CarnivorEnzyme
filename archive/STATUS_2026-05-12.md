## 1. Executive Summary

CarnivorEnzyme is a structural and thermodynamic atlas of convergently evolved digestive enzymes across all major carnivorous plant lineages. After a strategic literature review on 2026-05-12, the project's direction is **confirmed as scientifically sound** but **three corrections are being made** to the methods stack:

1. **Swap ESM-2 → ProSST + SaProt** as the primary protein language model evolutionary scorer.
2. **Add three newly-public genomes** (*Sarracenia purpurea*, *Pinguicula gigantea*, *N. mirabilis*) plus a `subgenome` polyploidy annotation.
3. **Cut FEP scope to ≤20 priority sites total**, strictly gated on the FoldX × ProSST quadrant.

The core scientific contribution — the **FoldX × evolutionary-constraint quadrant classification of convergent substitutions** — remains the novel analytical anchor and is unoccupied in the literature as of May 2026. The 2026 Albert/Fukushima *Trends in Genetics* review explicitly flags this question as open.

## 2. Scientific Framing

### The question

Carnivory evolved independently at least nine times within angiosperms. Each lineage co-opted pre-existing hydrolase gene families — chitinases, acid phosphatases, RNases, aspartic proteases, glutamic peptidases — for extracellular digestion. Fukushima et al. (2017, *Nat. Ecol. Evol.* 1:0059) established that the same amino acid substitutions recur at homologous alignment positions across phylogenetically independent lineages at a rate exceeding neutral expectation. **The structural and functional consequences of these substitutions remain uncharacterized.**

### What this project answers

For each convergent substitution in five enzyme families across nine independent origins:

1. Does the substitution alter protein stability? (FoldX ΔΔG at pH 2.5 / 3.5 / 5.0)
2. Is the substitution evolutionarily favored or tolerated? (ProSST + SaProt LLR; EVE/EVcouplings ΔΔE for deep-MSA families)
3. Does it shift substrate binding affinity? (alchemical FEP ΔΔG_bind for top sites)
4. Does it shift active-site pKa? (constant-pH MD)
5. Does it correlate with pitcher-fluid pH? (PDB2PQR + APBS electrostatic surface)
6. Does it track polyploidy / subgenome origin? *(new — added 2026-05-12)*
7. Does it converge functionally in PLM embedding space, not just at the site level? *(new — ACEP, Stiffler 2025 PNAS)*

### Novelty

The FoldX × evolutionary-constraint axis on convergent substitutions has not been published. The closest precedents:

- **DETANGO** (Ding et al., bioRxiv Feb 2026, doi:10.64898/2026.02.03.703560) — does stability/function decomposition with deep learning, but on general variants, not convergent evolution.
- **Chugunov et al. 2025** (*RSC Chem. Biol.* 6:975) — ran FoldX and EVmutation independently on a single endoglucanase and showed experimentally that they enrich different properties. Validates the orthogonal-axis premise; does not implement the quadrant.
- **Rubisco convergence paper** (bioRxiv 2025.10.08.681247) — FoldX on convergent sites in plants, but one enzyme family, FoldX-only, no evolutionary-constraint layer.

This project is the first to combine all axes on a multi-family multi-lineage convergence dataset.

## 6. The Three Pivot Corrections (2026-05-12)

### Correction 1 — ESM-2 → ProSST (+ SaProt secondary)

**Why:** ProteinGym leaderboard (May 2026) places **ProSST** (Li et al., NeurIPS 2024) as the first open model exceeding Spearman 0.5 on the full benchmark, top on stability/binding/expression subsets. It ingests quantized structure tokens — exactly what you have once AF3 runs. **SaProt** (Su et al., ICLR 2024) is co-top using Foldseek 3Di tokens. **ESM-2** is ~0.40 Spearman, no structure input. AlphaMissense is human-trained and is **not appropriate** for plant enzymes.

The two-axis architecture (structure-based FoldX + sequence-based evolutionary) is retained. Only the tool filling the evolutionary axis changes.

**Code impact:**
- Rename `workflow/scripts/score_esm2.py` → `score_prosst.py`. ESM-2 retained as sanity baseline.
- Add `workflow/scripts/score_saprot.py` for the structure-aware secondary axis.
- Update `workflow/envs/esm.yaml` → `workflow/envs/plm.yaml` with ProSST + SaProt PyPI deps.
- Rename `evmutation.smk` rule outputs and downstream `compare_foldx_evmutation.py` to operate on ProSST scores by default.

### Correction 2 — New genomes + polyploidy annotation

**Why:** Three genomes appeared between April 2025 and February 2026 that materially expand lineage coverage. The Albert/Fukushima 2026 *Trends in Genetics* review explicitly flagged the **polyploidy × convergence** question as open — which subgenome copies in *N. gracilis* (10x), *D. capensis* (12x), *D. muscipula* (4x) retain the convergent substitutions?

**Code impact:**
- Add three species to [config/species.yaml](config/species.yaml): *Sarracenia purpurea* (Ericales origin 5), *Pinguicula gigantea* (Lamiales origin 6), *Nepenthes mirabilis* (Caryophyllales origin 1).
- Add `subgenome` field to species.yaml schema for polyploid species. Values: `A`, `B`, `C`, … per Saul et al. 2023 subgenome dominance assignment.
- Update [enzyme_families.yaml](config/enzyme_families.yaml) entries to include subgenome tag per accession where the polyploid has multiple homeologs.

### Correction 3 — FEP scope cut to ≤20 sites total

**Why:** Initial budget implied ~1M ns of FEP simulation. RSC Chem. Sci. 2020 (Gapsys non-equilibrium) and our own equilibrium-MBAR estimate puts realistic per-site cost at ~390 ns total (13 λ × 5 ns × 3 reps × 2 legs). For ≤20 priority sites that is ~7,800 ns ≈ 5–15k GPU-hours — feasible on Hellbender. The previous open-ended scope was not.

**Gating rule:** Sites enter FEP only if they are in the **functional_gain** or **stability_function_tradeoff** quadrant AND ProSST LLR > 0 AND ancestral_aa ≠ Proline AND derived_aa ≠ Proline (pmx limitation).

**Code impact:**
- Hard cap `config.fep.max_targets: 20` (already in config.yaml; enforce in `run_fep.py`).
- Add tier ordering: Tier 1 families (5 sites each, 25 max → trim to 20) before Tier 2.

## 9. Open Strategic Questions

Items that need a decision and are not yet resolved:

1. **Wet-lab validation collaboration.** Citation analysis: Del Amo-Maestro 2022 (neprosin crystal, ~80 citations) vs. Butts et al. 2016 (Drosera proteases, computational-only, ~40 citations). One targeted biochemical experiment — expressing 2–3 ProSST/FoldX-quadrant-winner neprosin variants and measuring activity against gliadin peptide — would convert "predictions" to "predictions experimentally validated." The Goh lab (Malaysia, PRJEB86749) and Brodelius lab (Sweden) have done this assay for wild-type neprosin. **Decision needed:** initiate collaboration email or stay purely computational.

2. **Manuscript target.** *Nature Ecology & Evolution* (follow-up to Fukushima 2017) vs. *Molecular Biology and Evolution* vs. *eLife*. NEE is highest impact and topical-fit but slow; eLife allows faster open-review. **Decision needed:** when to commit, currently aiming for preprint on bioRxiv first regardless.

3. **Tier 2 inclusion.** Cysteine proteases (dionain), thaumatin-like proteins, GH17 β-glucanases — included or deferred to follow-up paper? Adds 5 families × 9 lineages of work. **Recommendation:** defer to v2 paper; document in Discussion as next-step.

4. **Subgenome resolution depth.** Per-homeolog FoldX is ~3–5× compute. **Recommendation:** run FoldX on all homeologs but FEP/CpHMD only on the dominant subgenome copy.
